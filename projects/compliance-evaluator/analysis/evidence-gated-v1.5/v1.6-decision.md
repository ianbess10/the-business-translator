# Evidence-Gated Decision Pipeline v1.6 — Decision Record

## Decision

**Proceed later with a separately versioned v1.6 that preserves the approved Coverage and Evidence Rulebook, introduces independently adjudicated benchmark authority, and composes mapping coverage and evidence condition as concurrent deterministic assurance dimensions. Adverse operating evidence must retain its remediation and escalation consequence even when mapping is partial. Do not modify, tune, rerun or rescore v1.5.**

This record selects governance, architecture and release controls only. It does not adjudicate labels, create a new dataset, implement v1.6, change the rulebook, call a provider, execute regression cases, score results or prepare a holdout.

## Why a new version is warranted

v1.5 achieved its architectural objective: mapping completeness was calculated from approved coverage assertions rather than inferred by a model, evidence observations were bounded to approved requirements, and every case completed without quarantine or retry.

The regression then exposed two distinct issues:

- four frozen mapping labels conflict with the approved two-element rulebook; and
- one correctly observed adverse operating indicator was suppressed by the policy's partial-coverage precedence.

Changing the rulebook calculator to reproduce the labels would subordinate approved business authority to a benchmark. Ignoring the `CON-011` escalation miss would leave a material operating-risk defect. v1.6 must resolve each through the correct control channel.

## Required pre-implementation adjudication

Before v1.6 implementation, a qualified reviewer independent of workflow coding must adjudicate `CON-006`, `CON-007`, `CON-011` and `CON-012` using:

- the source-supported atomic obligation;
- the approved obligation elements;
- frozen control-design descriptions;
- approved coverage assertions and their rationale; and
- the original label rationale.

The reviewer must decide whether each frozen complete-mapping label or the approved rulebook is authoritative. The record must state the reasoning, reviewer identity and role, decision date and source artefacts.

The existing `SA-REG-BASELINE-001` v1.0 labels remain immutable. If adjudication approves different expected states, publish a separately versioned dataset and evaluator baseline. Preserve both versions and report results against each authority explicitly; never represent a new-label score as the original v1.5 regression score.

## Selected v1.6 architecture

### 1. Dual-axis assurance state

Maintain two independent deterministic dimensions for every entered assurance case:

- **coverage state**: complete, partial, absent or not assessable; and
- **evidence condition**: sufficient, design deficiency, missing operating evidence, adverse operating evidence or not assessable.

Neither dimension may overwrite the other. The terminal record must retain both states, their requirement or element references, owners and actions.

If compatibility requires one headline `assurance_outcome`, derive it only after both dimensions and their actions are preserved. The headline is a presentation choice, not a loss of operational information.

### 2. Concurrent remediation routing

Derive remediation independently by condition:

- partial coverage → mapping review routed to the accountable mapping owner;
- design deficiency → design remediation routed to the control owner;
- missing operating evidence → evidence request routed to the evidence owner; and
- adverse operating evidence → operating remediation routed to the control and oversight owners.

A case may carry more than one action. Deduplicate identical recipients and actions deterministically, but do not suppress a valid action because another assurance issue exists.

### 3. Adverse-evidence escalation floor

An adverse indicator governed by an approved high-severity rule must set the operating condition to adverse, retain operating remediation and trigger its configured escalation roles regardless of whether coverage is complete or partial.

Coverage gaps may add mapping review or escalation, but cannot lower the adverse-evidence severity or remove its escalation. This is a general rule driven by requirement class and approved severity—not by a benchmark case ID.

The v1.0 rulebook identifies adverse-indicator requirements but does not itself define severity or escalation routing. Before v1.6 freeze, a separately versioned and human-approved rulebook or policy overlay must supply those attributes. They may not be inferred from labels or evidence prose.

### 4. Rulebook remains authoritative business data

The v1.0 rulebook remains unchanged unless a separate human governance process versions it. v1.6 must continue to:

- calculate coverage without a model;
- prohibit case IDs, label content and case-specific exceptions in the rulebook;
- quarantine missing coverage authority instead of inferring it; and
- isolate supplied evidence from mapping membership.

### 5. Evaluator identity and authority disclosure

The separately versioned evaluator must derive its displayed workflow version from the evaluated payload and assert consistency across run ID, workflow version, evaluation ID, comparison ID and output path.

Every score must identify the dataset and label-authority version. A mismatch must fail evaluation before writing a report. This fixes the inherited `v1.4` display defect prospectively; v1.5 is not rescored.

## Controls retained without relaxation

v1.6 will retain:

- approved-obligation authority and source-stage bypass;
- deterministic rulebook mapping with zero mapping model calls;
- bounded evidence observations against supplied evidence and approved requirements;
- current controls separated from remediation candidates;
- catalogue owner resolution;
- append-only stage evidence before validation;
- fail-closed quarantine and complete terminal-case accounting;
- zero retry, repair, fallback and manual correction;
- mandatory human review and `not_determined` compliance conclusion;
- provider-subset audit and certification for every changed provider contract;
- runner isolation from evaluator labels;
- quarantine-aware scoring; and
- no holdout unless every applicable gate passes.

## Required offline tests before certification and freeze

The future implementation must prove:

1. coverage and evidence states are retained independently for complete, partial and absent mappings;
2. missing evidence remains visible and routable when coverage is partial;
3. adverse operating evidence cannot be suppressed by partial or absent coverage;
4. concurrent mapping and operating remediation actions are both retained;
5. escalation is the union of independently applicable approved escalation rules;
6. action and role deduplication does not remove distinct responsibilities;
7. unknown rulebook authority still quarantines without inference;
8. all 24 frozen input paths terminate without runner access to labels;
9. synthetic boundary fixtures cover combinations not present in the benchmark;
10. evaluator metadata rejects workflow, dataset or authority-version inconsistency;
11. the evaluator self-test distinguishes original and adjudicated label authorities; and
12. adverse severity and escalation are loaded only from approved versioned policy authority; and
13. case-identity scanning finds no case-specific runtime or rulebook logic.

## Provider certification decision

If the source and evidence provider contracts, prompts, wrapper, endpoint and model hashes remain exact, their existing certifications may be inherited. Dual-axis composition, remediation union and escalation precedence are deterministic and require offline boundary tests rather than provider calls.

Any change to a provider-facing schema, prompt or request wrapper invalidates inheritance and requires a generic non-benchmark provider certification before freeze.

## Prohibited responses

v1.6 must not:

- modify, rerun or rescore v1.5;
- silently change the v1.0 dataset or labels;
- tune runtime policy to the four failed case IDs;
- weaken the approved rulebook to improve the original benchmark score;
- treat evaluator labels as operating authority;
- allow partial coverage to suppress adverse evidence or mandatory escalation;
- collapse concurrent conditions before preserving their actions and owners;
- inherit certification after any provider-contract hash change;
- repair or retry invalid provider responses; or
- proceed to holdout after a failed release gate.
